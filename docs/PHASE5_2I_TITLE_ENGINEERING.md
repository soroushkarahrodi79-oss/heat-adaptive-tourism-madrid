## 4. Three finalists

**Finalist 1 (conservative, journal-safe) — A2**
*Screening urban tourism opportunities under extreme heat: a constraint-first
decision-support approach*
- **Why it works:** unambiguous tourism-decision-support identity; names the
  architecture; reads as a standard TMP empirical-method article; very low overclaim
  risk.
- **Main weakness:** does not foreground the empirical hook (that thermal method
  changes the decision), so it under-sells the paper's most distinctive result.
- **Positioning:** the safest TMP submission title; also fits Sustainable Cities and
  Society and Urban Climate with no change.

**Finalist 2 (thermal-method sensitivity / stronger conceptual) — B2 [recommended base]**
*Thermal representation as a decision variable in heat-adaptive tourism opportunity
screening*
- **Why it works:** states the paper's actual contribution (thermal-method choice is a
  decision variable) in claim-safe, neutral language; distinctive; resists both
  routing and pure-methods pattern-matching; tourism-native via "tourism opportunity
  screening."
- **Main weakness:** "thermal representation" is mildly abstract for a general tourism
  reader; carries no place bound on its own.
- **Positioning:** ideal for TMP's stated appetite for introducing and critiquing
  methods; strong at SCS.

**Finalist 3 (method-sensitivity hook) — B1**
*When the thermal method changes the decision: heat-adaptive tourism opportunity
screening*
- **Why it works:** highest memorability among the accurate titles; foregrounds the
  empirical finding while the subtitle keeps the tourism-screening identity.
- **Main weakness:** the "When … changes the decision" framing is slightly assertive
  and journalistic; a cautious reviewer could read momentum toward "the method that
  matters," though the text keeps it neutral.
- **Positioning:** TMP or SCS; the most engaging option if a stronger hook is wanted.

## 5. Recommended title

**Thermal representation as a decision variable in heat-adaptive tourism opportunity
screening: evidence from a Madrid pilot**  *(16 words)*

(Base = Finalist 2 / B2, with a bounding subtitle.)

**Why (≤120 words):** It states the paper's genuine contribution — thermal-method
choice as a *decision variable* — in the charter's own neutral language, so it cannot
be read as claiming physical-model superiority (both directions of change; no
accuracy claim). "Heat-adaptive tourism opportunity screening" keeps a clear tourism/
destination-management identity and signals the upstream screening object without the
word "routing," so it resists the R4 pattern-match. "Evidence from a Madrid pilot"
bounds the empirical claim, signals the applied case-study character TMP tolerates,
and avoids any city-wide implication. It is accurate with the dashboard and the HATI
acronym removed. Among finalists it is the best compromise of accuracy, tourism
identity, distinctiveness, and claim safety.

## 6. Strongest rejected title

**B3 — *Does thermal representation change tourism-feasibility decisions under extreme
heat? A Madrid pilot*** (mean 8.22). It is safe, clearly tourism, method-sensitivity-
focused, and already Madrid-bounded, and it was the closest non-finalist. It lost to
B2 on two counts: the interrogative form is more tentative than the declarative
"decision variable" framing and slightly weakens the contribution signal, and B2 with
its bounding subtitle already captures the Madrid pilot while reading as a stronger,
more distinctive statement. (Family C's C1, *Screening before routing…*, is the most
conceptually tempting rejection overall but was cut earlier for foregrounding
"routing" and inviting the pattern-match the paper must avoid.)

## 7. Should "Madrid" appear in the title?

**Yes — only in the subtitle, as "evidence from a Madrid pilot."** This bounds the
empirical claim and signals a case study, matching the manuscript's single-pilot scope.
The forms "… in Madrid" (risks a city-wide reading) and a bare place name in the main
title are avoided; "a Madrid pilot" makes the limited coverage explicit.

## TITLE GATE

The recommended title breaches no claim ceiling (thermal representation framed as a
decision variable, not a superiority claim); carries a clear tourism/decision-support
identity; implies no physical-model superiority; contains no routing confusion; uses no
product/dashboard framing; accurately represents the manuscript with implementation
detail removed; and reads naturally for Tourism Management Perspectives.

**TITLE SELECTED**
