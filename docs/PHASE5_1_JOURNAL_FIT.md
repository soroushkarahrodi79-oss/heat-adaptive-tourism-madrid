# PHASE5_1_JOURNAL_FIT.md — HATI-Madrid Phase 5.1

Version 1.0 · 2026-08-18. Journal audit (Task 7) and journal-specific positioning
test (Task 8). Uses current official aims/scope. **Impact factor is not a
decision criterion; no acceptance probabilities are invented.** The selection
principle is *which venue requires the least distortion of the actual evidence*
(single day, no field validation, no behavioural claim, geospatial decision-
support with a tourism application).

---

## 1. Audited journals

### Tourism Management Perspectives (TMP, Elsevier)
- **Scope:** interdisciplinary planning/management of tourism; "socio-cultural,
  technological, planning and policy aspects"; **"particularly welcomes papers
  seeking to introduce new research methods and critiquing existing ones."**
- **Thematic fit:** strong — tourism decision-support is native.
- **Methodological fit:** strong — a methods contribution + critique of an
  existing method (proxy vs physical; constraint-first vs composite) is explicitly
  invited.
- **Single-destination tolerance:** good (applied/regional studies are routine).
- **Theoretical-contribution expectation:** moderate — will want a management/
  conceptual takeaway, not only a pipeline.
- **Decision-support/technology fit:** good. **Geospatial-method fit:** acceptable
  but not its core strength (reviewers may be non-GIS).
- **No-field-validation risk:** moderate — tourism reviewers less fixated on
  Tmrt/UTCI ground truth than biometeorology reviewers.
- **Single-day-scope risk:** moderate — framed as a demonstration, defensible.
- **Format:** empirical research articles; standard Elsevier length.

### Journal of Destination Marketing & Management (JDMM, Elsevier)
- **Scope:** marketing and management of destinations; "robust theoretical
  understanding of destination marketing and management."
- **Fit:** **weak.** The paper offers no marketing, branding, demand, or
  destination-management *theory*, and makes no behavioural claim — the journal's
  centre of gravity. High rejection risk on scope. **Not recommended.**

### Tourism Geographies (Taylor & Francis)
- **Scope:** critical, theoretically rigorous human-geography of tourism; social-
  science lens (lists geospatial sciences but demands critical/theoretical
  scholarship).
- **Fit:** **weak–moderate.** A quantitative GIS decision-support method paper
  without a critical-theory or human-geography contribution is a poor match; would
  require reframing toward critical scholarship the evidence does not support.
  **Not recommended as primary.**

### Sustainable Cities and Society (SCS, Elsevier) — *added candidate*
- **Scope:** environmentally sustainable, socially resilient cities; scope
  **explicitly lists "Decision Support Systems for trade-off and uncertainty
  analysis for improved management of cities."**
- **Thematic fit:** strong for the *architecture*; tourism is an application
  domain, not the journal's core.
- **Methodological/geospatial fit:** very strong; hosts the closest adjacent
  thermal-accessibility work and SOLWEIG city-mapping.
- **Single-day / no-field-validation tolerance:** good for applied UTCI studies,
  but selectivity is high and reviewers may push for stronger novelty or a
  validation gesture.
- **Distortion required:** reframe tourism as an application; **no distortion of
  evidence.**

### Urban Climate (Elsevier) — *added candidate*
- **Scope:** theory, science, and **applications** relevant to urban climatic
  conditions; explicitly serves "decision-making communities."
- **Fit:** strong methodological home for a single-city, single-episode applied
  SOLWEIG/UTCI method-sensitivity study; routinely publishes exactly this scope
  and tolerates no-field-validation with honest caveats.
- **Risk:** tourism framing is thin — may read as urban-climate methods with a
  tourism application (arguably the paper's most honest identity).

*(Journal of Sustainable Tourism was considered and set aside: good for
climate-adaptation framing but expects a sustainability/behavioural or policy
contribution the paper does not make.)*

## 2. Ranking

### PRIMARY TARGET — Tourism Management Perspectives
The venue where the paper's **stated identity** (tourism heat-suitability
decision-support) is native *and* whose scope explicitly invites new methods and
critiques of existing ones — exactly the proxy-vs-physical + constraint-first-vs-
composite contribution. It tolerates applied single-destination studies and
technological/planning content, and its reviewers are least likely to demand
Tmrt/UTCI field validation. It requires the least distortion of the paper's
framing while fully accepting its evidence. **Chosen not on impact factor but on
lowest-distortion fit to what the paper actually claims.**

### SECONDARY TARGET — Sustainable Cities and Society
Near-exact scope match for the *architecture* ("decision support for trade-off and
uncertainty analysis"); strongest methodological/geospatial home; hosts the
closest adjacent literature. Requires reframing tourism as an application domain
(mild framing shift, **no** evidence distortion). Higher selectivity and possible
pressure for a validation gesture make it secondary rather than primary.

### SAFER TARGET — Urban Climate
Lowest bar for the paper's specific evidence ceiling: single-city, single-episode
applied UTCI/SOLWEIG studies with transparent methods and explicit no-field-
validation are standard here, and the method-sensitivity result is squarely in
scope. "Safer" in the sense of *least friction with the evidence limitations* —
the cost is that the tourism framing must be lightened, moving the paper toward
its urban-climate-methods identity.

## 3. Positioning test (Task 8) — top three

### Tourism Management Perspectives
- **One-sentence framing:** "A transparent, constraint-first decision-support
  method for screening urban tourism opportunities under extreme heat shows that
  the choice of thermal method materially changes which options remain feasible."
- **Likely objection:** "What is the management/theoretical contribution beyond a
  pipeline, and can you act on it without validated inputs?"
- **Emphasise:** decision-support value (H3/H4), governance relevance (Annals 2026),
  transparency vs composite indices.
- **De-emphasise:** raster/SOLWEIG implementation detail; move to supplementary.

### Sustainable Cities and Society
- **One-sentence framing:** "A constraint-first, uncertainty-aware urban decision-
  support architecture that couples physical thermal-exposure modelling to
  opportunity screening, demonstrating method-choice sensitivity in a real
  extreme-heat episode."
- **Likely objection:** "Novelty vs existing UTCI-accessibility/decision-support
  work, and single-day scope without validation."
- **Emphasise:** the decision-support-under-uncertainty architecture (C8–C13),
  the proxy-vs-physical sensitivity, reproducibility.
- **De-emphasise:** tourism-management framing; keep tourism as the application.

### Urban Climate
- **One-sentence framing:** "In a real Madrid extreme-heat episode, physically
  based UTCI and a simple air-temperature/tree-count proxy disagree on a material,
  time-concentrated share of pedestrian heat-suitability classifications, with
  direct consequences for an auditable screening decision."
- **Likely objection:** "Is the tourism application essential, or is this an
  urban-climate method with a tourism veneer?"
- **Emphasise:** the physical method-sensitivity result (H1/H2), spatial/temporal
  UTCI structure, honest validation limits.
- **De-emphasise:** the scenario/tourism-screening narrative relative to the
  thermal-method finding.

**Least-distortion verdict:** TMP distorts *framing* least (keeps the tourism
decision-support identity, accepts the evidence as-is); Urban Climate distorts
*evidence expectations* least (single-day/no-validation are native) but pulls the
identity toward urban-climate methods; SCS sits between. TMP is primary because it
is the only venue where **neither the evidence nor the paper's stated purpose has
to bend** — the tourism-methods contribution is exactly what its scope solicits.
