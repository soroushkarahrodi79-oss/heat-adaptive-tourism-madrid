## Step 1 — Extreme heat is increasingly a tourism-*management* problem
- **Claim (bounded):** extreme heat is a recurring summer hazard in Southern
  Europe that concentrates on the times/places tourism uses, and existing heat
  governance rarely accounts for visitors.
- **Evidence:** heat risk action planning for tourism (Annals of Tourism Research
  2026 — "existing Heat Action Plans rarely account for tourism"); OECD Tourism
  Trends 2026 (adapting tourism to extreme weather); Southern-Europe adaptation
  (Sustainability 2026); Madrid/Sevilla plaza comfort (Env Sci Pollut Res 2022);
  tourism exposure gridded dataset (ERL 2024).
- **Defensible?** Yes. **Narrowing:** framed as a *management/screening* problem,
  not a behavioural or mortality claim (mortality is background, not ours).

## Step 2 — Conventional climate-tourism metrics lack intra-urban, time-specific decision resolution
- **Claim:** the dominant tourism-suitability tradition is the weighted composite
  climate index, which operates at coarse spatial/temporal scale and is sensitive
  to its own weights/thresholds, so it cannot say *which microsite is feasible at
  which hour*.
- **Evidence:** HCI/TCI inter-comparison (Atmosphere 2016); reliability/usability
  of tourism climate indices (Earth Perspectives 2016); recent HCI/TCI
  applications (Int J Biometeorol 2025); GIS-AHP tourism suitability (2011) as the
  weighted-MCDA exemplar.
- **Defensible?** Yes — the weight-sensitivity critique is well established in this
  literature.

## Step 3 — Physical thermal models capture radiant heterogeneity but are rarely connected transparently to tourism-opportunity screening
- **Claim:** SOLWEIG-class models resolve the radiant, shade-driven heterogeneity
  that air-temperature and simple vegetation proxies miss, but are typically used
  for design/mapping, not wired into an auditable tourism-opportunity decision.
- **Evidence:** SOLWEIG 1.0 (2008); WRF-UCM-SOLWEIG mapping (Sustainable Cities and
  Society 2024); Tmrt validation ranges (Urban Climate 2019); "Beyond LST" (2026,
  LST ≠ comfort). HATI link: the proxy-vs-physical result (C1–C4) shows the
  connection *matters* — method choice changes 33.3% of classifications.
- **Defensible?** Yes. **Narrowing:** we claim the *connection is under-served and
  consequential*, not that the physical model is validated.

## Step 4 — Heat-aware routing/accessibility is advancing fast but operates downstream of candidate selection
- **Claim:** the 2025–2026 routing/accessibility wave optimises paths or measures
  reachability but presupposes the destination/candidate set; the upstream
  *screening* question is unanswered.
- **Evidence:** Cool Routes (Building and Environment 2026); CoolWalks (Scientific
  Reports 2025); UTCI-adjusted pedestrian accessibility (Sustainable Cities and
  Society 2026); sidewalk heat-risk (Environment and Planning B 2025). See the
  boundary table in `docs/PHASE5_1_NOVELTY_AUDIT.md` §3.
- **Defensible?** Yes — verified: these works take destinations/candidates as
  given. This is the paper's sharpest positioning and must be explicit.

## Step 5 — Therefore: a gap for auditable, uncertainty-aware tourism-opportunity screening
- **Claim (the gap):** no established method screens tourism opportunities by
  explicit physical-thermal + accessibility + evidence constraints at a specific
  hour, keeps hazard/exposure/comfort/evidence traceably separate, propagates
  uncertainty into categorical confidence, and returns surviving alternatives (or
  an explicit none) — without a behavioural claim.
- **Evidence:** the absence established across Steps 2–4 + the novelty audit
  (no DIRECT precedent); the constraint-first architecture (C13) and the
  method-sensitivity finding (C1–C4) as what fills it.
- **Defensible?** Yes, conditional on Step 4's boundary holding — which it does.
  **Narrowing:** "gap" is stated as an *under-served defensible question*, per
  `docs/RESEARCH_GAP.md`, never as "nobody combined these datasets".
