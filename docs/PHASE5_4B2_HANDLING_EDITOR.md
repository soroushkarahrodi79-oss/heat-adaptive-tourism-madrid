Desk assessment of `manuscript/MANUSCRIPT_TMP_v0.2.md` for *Tourism Management Perspectives*.
I set aside any affection for the project and ask only whether this belongs in TMP peer review.

## Decision: SEND TO REVIEW — with major reservations (desk-reject risk is real)

The topic is timely, the data are open and honestly handled, the claim discipline is
unusually good, and the references are verified. That clears the automatic-desk-reject bar.
But the manuscript's centre of gravity is a spatial decision-support method, and its
tourism-management contribution — the thing TMP exists to publish — is its weakest element.
I will send it to review, but I expect reviewers to demand a stronger management framing,
and I would not be surprised if a co-editor desk-rejected it as a better fit for
*Sustainable Cities and Society* or *Urban Climate*.

## Answers to the desk questions
1. *Title intelligible to TMP readers?* Partly. "Thermal representation as a decision
   variable" is method jargon; "heat-adaptive tourism opportunity screening" carries the
   tourism signal. Acceptable but not inviting for a management audience.
2. *Abstract communicates a management contribution?* It communicates a *finding* (method
   choice changes classifications) and an architecture; the *management* payoff is implicit.
3. *Fits TMP identity strongly enough?* Marginally. This is the core risk.
4. *8,746 main-text words too long?* On the long side for TMP; Methods (2,818) is heavy for
   the contribution and could shed detail to the supplement.
5. *Incremental?* The method-sensitivity finding is a fresh, narrow point; the architecture
   is conventional rule-based screening. Net: modest novelty, honestly bounded.
6. *Introduction sharp enough?* Well-structured but slow to reach the management stakes; the
   "why a manager should care" arrives late.
7. *Clear "so what?" for managers?* Not sharp enough — the single biggest desk concern.
8. *Looks like an engineering demonstrator?* At times (architecture schematic, engine-state
   vocabulary). The tourism reader must be led, not assumed.
9. *Four figures + three tables appropriate?* Yes, proportionate and clean.
10. *Strong enough to survive desk review despite one city / one day / no field / no
    behavioural validation?* Yes, *if* the science claim stays at its current disciplined
    ceiling and the management contribution is sharpened. The bounded scope is not
    disqualifying on its own for TMP Perspectives (which tolerates applied pilots), but the
    thin management payoff plus the method-heavy framing together are what put desk-rejection
    on the table.
